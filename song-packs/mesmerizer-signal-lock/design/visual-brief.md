# Visual Brief: Mesmerizer Signal Lock

## Core Direction

- Surface mood: bright, pop, cool, high-contrast, and polished.
- Underlayer: controlled distress, signal lock, and hypnotic repetition should be felt underneath the bright surface.
- Do not use lyrics text, audio files, screenshots, external textures, or third-party visual assets.

## Screen Composition

- Intro: wide daytime cyber live venue. Establish the bright stage and huge displays.
- Verse A: close two-motif composition. The focus/sync cards should occupy most of the screen and trade attention cleanly.
- Verse B: keep the close two-motif structure, but let a small SOS/no-signal motif pull the eye away for short interruptions.
- Verse lock/pre-chorus: push the huge display and hypnosis eye forward, then pull the camera back into the stage.
- Chorus A: wide concert composition. Open the full venue with lighting, audience glow, LED walls, and camera movement.
- Post-chorus: reduce subject density with bright afterimage space before the interlude.
- Interlude: the biggest identity change. The main display becomes a control/signal surface with scan bars, SOS/HELP, and no-signal blocks.
- Rebuild: reassemble the control surface back into bright show graphics.
- Chorus B: widest, brightest show composition, with the hidden lock layer more visible than Chorus A.
- Outro: pull back and settle into the bright surface again, leaving a thin fading signal.
- Scene identity should not rely only on camera distance. Add large 3D overlay
  layers when useful: lock rings for fixation, a scan curtain for interlude,
  side ribbons for chorus, and pale afterimage planes for post-chorus/outro.

## Verse Motifs

- Primary motif: two large focus/sync cards with hypnosis-eye markings. They should read as bright pop-stage graphic props, not as generic UI panels.
- Secondary motif: a small SOS/no-signal display that interrupts the clean surface as a hidden distress layer.
- Motion: beat changes the target sharply, but the objects follow with spring, damping, lag, and overshoot so the motion stays lively instead of robotic.
- Keep the verse simple: two main focus/sync motifs plus one signal motif. Do not add more competing symbols.

## Chorus Motifs

- The live venue becomes the subject.
- Chorus A: make LEDs, spotlights, audience glow sticks, and camera energy wider and brighter.
- Chorus B: same structure, stronger density and light response.

## Data Inputs

- Songle beat: target changes, light hits, LED cuts, audience motion.
- Songle chorus: wide venue energy and lighting density.
- Songle melody: available as timing density data for this pack, but not treated as a pitch curve.
- Songle chord/repeat data: used only as structure guidance, not as a direct visual template.
- `emotion-map.json`: song-owned time map for energy, motion, tension, brightness, release, and interpretive vocal gesture cues. It is based on Songle structure plus human interpretation, not audio waveform analysis.
- `composition-map.json`: song-owned time map for camera distance, screen occupancy, main subject, display mode, and viewer attention. It is based on visual planning and public-reception checks, not audio waveform analysis.
- Stage text should avoid production labels such as LIVE, STAGE, SCREEN, LIGHT, or BEAM. Use small in-world state displays such as FOCUS, SYNC, WAIT, RETRY, SOS, NO SIG, LOCK, and 000.

## Safety

- Song-specific decisions stay in this song pack.
- General Songle analysis lessons may be shared with system docs, but this song's look must not become a template for other songs.
